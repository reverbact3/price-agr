The problem

Millions of families can't count on having enough food

Food insecurity means a household doesn't always have reliable access to enough food for an active, healthy life, usually because money runs out. It's more common than most people expect.

The numbers

13.5% of U.S. households were food insecure at some point in 2023 — about 18 million households. USDA ERS, 2023 data
47.4M people lived in those food-insecure households, including millions of children. USDA ERS, 2023 data
5.1% of households had very low food security, meaning people actually ate less or skipped meals. USDA ERS, 2023 data
673M people worldwide faced hunger in 2024, roughly 1 in 12. UN SOFI report, 2025

It's been getting worse at home

The share of U.S. households facing food insecurity dropped during pandemic-era support, then climbed as food prices rose.

What the levels mean

The USDA sorts households into levels. Food insecurity isn't only about going hungry. It also covers the stress and trade-offs that come before that.

Food secure

The household can reliably get enough food all year.

Low food security

People cut quality and variety, buy cheaper and less healthy food, but don't usually eat less.

Very low food security

People skip meals or eat less because there isn't enough money for food.

Where grocery prices come in

Prices vary a lot

The same dozen eggs can cost very different amounts at stores a few minutes apart.

Comparing takes time

Checking five store apps and weekly flyers is time most busy families don't have.

Cheap isn't a meal

A list of low prices doesn't tell you what to cook. People need a plan, not a spreadsheet.

How it works

A price comparer and a meal planner in one

Most stores don't share prices in a way that's easy to compare. We collect them, line up the same items, and turn the cheapest options into meals you can actually cook.

Four steps

STEP 1

Gather prices

We pull current prices and product details from at least five grocery stores around Bridgeport.

STEP 2

Compare fairly

We match the same item across stores and compare by unit price (per ounce or per pound), so a bigger package doesn't look cheaper than it is.

STEP 3

Plan the meals

You set a budget and any diet needs. The planner suggests meals built from the cheapest ingredients and creates a shopping list.

STEP 4

Shop and save

Split the list across stores for the lowest total, or pick one store and see its best-priced version of your list.

Quick term: unit price

The price per ounce, pound or count. A $4 bag of 2 lb rice costs $2/lb, while a $7 bag of 5 lb costs $1.40/lb. Unit prices are how the app compares fairly.

The tricky parts

Sales and loyalty prices

Some prices need a store card or only last a week. We record the regular price, the sale price and the end date, so a plan doesn't rely on an expired deal.

Keeping prices fresh

Prices are re-checked on a schedule, and each one shows when it was last checked. Older prices get flagged.

Near-matches

A 12 oz bag and a 16 oz bag are the same product in different sizes. When the matcher isn't confident, the item goes to a review list instead of being guessed.

Extra trips cost something

Saving $1 isn't worth a second drive. That's why the "Up to 2 stores" and "One store only" options exist.

Roadmap

Built in order, one phase at a time

We get the core working first, then add features, then polish. That keeps the project realistic for a capstone timeline.

PHASE 1IN PLANNING

Working comparer + simple interface

The minimum that proves the idea works.

  • Prices from five or more local stores
  • Item matching and unit-price comparison
  • A search and meal-planning screen anyone can use
PHASE 2

More features

Once the core is reliable.

  • More stores and neighborhoods
  • Smarter meal suggestions by diet and budget
  • Saved lists and favorite meals
PHASE 3

Polish

The details that make it feel finished.

  • Faster loading and cleaner design
  • Mobile app experience
  • Feedback from real shoppers built in

How we'll know it works

Real savings

Optimized baskets cost less than shopping one store for the same list.

Fresh prices

Prices are recent enough that what you see is what you pay.

Easy to use

Testers can build a meal plan without help.

Challenges we expect

Stores update prices often, and the same product can have different names at each store. Matching items correctly is the hardest technical part, so it's the first thing we're tackling.
